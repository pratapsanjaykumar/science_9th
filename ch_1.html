Class 9 CBSE
NCERT Page No. 4 Pause and Ponder Q1

Question 1. Think of a prediction you or your family made recently (for example, the outcome of a cricket match). Was it based on evidence and reasoning or solely on guesswork? How can scientific thinking improve such predictions?

Answer:

Most everyday predictions are often based on guesswork rather than evidence. For example, a family member might say, "India will win the cricket match today because it has strong players." This is mainly a general belief and is not based on detailed analysis.

Scientific thinking improves such predictions by using evidence, past performance data, pitch conditions, weather, and player statistics. For instance, checking a team’s recent form or a player’s performance on a particular ground makes the prediction more logical and reliable.

NCERT Page No. 6 Pause and Ponder Q2

Question 2. Describe one situation where an approximate answer is good enough, and one where you would need a very exact value.

Answer:

  • Approximate Answer Situation: Estimating travel time (e.g., “about 30 minutes”) or daily needs like food quantity in cooking where slight variation does not cause harm.
  • Exact Value Situation: Measuring medicine doses or constructing a bridge, where exact numerical precision is crucial for safety and correctness.
NCERT Page No. 6 Pause and Ponder Q3

Question 3. Choose a real-life object (maybe a pressure cooker or a mobile phone) or a problem (maybe a traffic jam near your school). Make a sketch listing what kind of ideas from physics, chemistry, biology, earth science, or mathematics are involved. Show how at least two branches of science connect with your example.

Pressure Cooker Model
Interdisciplinary Connection Model:

Everyday objects like a pressure cooker or mobile phone demonstrate how physical thermodynamic laws intersect with chemical reactions and mathematical measurements.

Answer:

Example 1: Pressure Cooker
  • Physics: Heat and pressure increase inside the cooker, raising the boiling point of water and cooking food faster.
  • Chemistry: Cooking involves chemical changes in food, breaking complex molecules to make them softer and easier to digest.
  • Mathematics: Used in measuring time, temperature, and quantity of ingredients.

Connection: Physics and chemistry are connected. Increased pressure (physics) speeds up chemical changes in food (chemistry), resulting in faster cooking.

Example 2: Mobile Phone
  • Physics: Electricity, circuits, sound waves, and electromagnetic waves for wireless communication.
  • Chemistry: Battery functionality through chemical redox reactions that store and supply electrical energy.
  • Mathematics: Used in coding, data processing, and signal calculations.

Connection: Chemical energy in the battery (chemistry) is converted into electrical energy (physics) to operate the device.

Example 3: Traffic Jam near School
  • Physics: Vehicle acceleration, deceleration, and tyre-road friction determining stopping distance.
  • Mathematics: Statistical calculations of average speed and traffic density flow patterns during peak hours.
  • Earth Science: Vehicle exhaust producing air pollutants, forming smog and contributing to atmospheric impact.

Connection: Car motion patterns (physics) enable traffic flow predictions using equations and graphs (mathematics).

NCERT Page No. 2 Activity 1.1: Let Us Model

Question 1. Suppose you ride a bicycle from your school to your home. You want to model the time it takes to go home from school. What details would you keep? What details could you ignore? Suggest why ignoring some details may actually be useful?

Answer:

To model the time taken to travel from school to home on a bicycle, important factors such as the distance between school and home, average speed of the bicycle, total time taken, and road conditions (if significantly affecting speed) need to be considered.

Less important factors like the color of the bicycle, type of clothes worn, surroundings like roadside buildings or trees, or the brand of the bicycle can be safely ignored.

Why ignoring details is useful: Simplifying the situation removes unnecessary variables, allowing us to focus entirely on core mathematical and physical factors determining travel time.
